The weekend

What you'll actually do with other creative humans

None of it happens on a screen. Some sessions are guided, some are yours to wander into. Nothing is mandatory except leaving your phone in its box.

Friday

Afternoon Drawing

Grab a pencil and a pad and spend the afternoon drawing whatever is in front of you. Every level welcome. We close the weekend with a relaxed presentation where everyone shows what they made. No critique, just sharing.

Lake Loop Run

A 1 mile group circle run around the lake. Easy pace, everyone stays together, walkers welcome.

Game Night

We'll bring the games.

Saturday Afternoon

Kayaking

Meditation

Short guided sits to quiet the part of your brain still reaching for a phone that isn't there.

The offline pledge

Your phone gets its own box.

We don't take your phone. When you check in Friday at four, you get a phone box that is yours to keep. Put your phone inside, tape it shut, and leave it in your room until checkout Sunday at eleven.

That is 43 hours off the grid. On the last morning we open the boxes together, and most people are surprised how little they reached for it.

FRI 4:00 PM  box it at check-in SUN 11:00 AM  open at check-out = 43 HOURS  offline

You won't need it anyway. The town is one street, straight down the road, and we hand you a paper map when you arrive. No GPS required. It is an honor system, not a lockup, so if you truly need it, it is right there in your room.

What you'll get

Nostalgic for your own instincts?

Two nights offline. One real reset for your creative brain, your hands, and your business.

This isn't just a nice trip. It's built to knock the rust off, get you making things again, and send you home with work and a few people who make things too.

You'll leave Peaks of Otter with:

Where did the love go?

You make things on a screen all day, and somewhere in there it quietly turned into a job. Two days with no undo button reminds you why you started. Nothing between you and the work, nothing buffering. Most people are surprised how fast the itch comes back once the screen is gone.

People who actually get it

Making things for a living is lonely work. You spend your days alone with a screen, and a group chat is not the same as talking to someone who understands the specific weirdness of the job. This is the opposite of that. A small group, phones off, most of a weekend around a fire and a shared table. No name tags, no elevator pitches, nobody selling you anything. Day one is a little awkward. By day two you have inside jokes. You leave with a handful of people who make things too, the kind who text back, trade work, and still know your name a year from now. For a lot of people this is the part they did not see coming, and the part they can't stop talking about after.

Don't scroll past another weekend

You don't leave with a notebook full of good intentions. You leave with actual pieces you cut and built by hand, ready to frame, gift, or hang on the wall the day you get home. Proof you spent the weekend making something instead of scrolling past it.

A genuine rest

Most trips send you home more tired than when you left, because the phone tagged along. Not this one. Kayaking, yoga, a slow loop around the lake, hammocks, and real stretches of nothing on the schedule. Forty-three hours off the grid does something a long weekend on your phone never will. You come back full, not fried.

Good questions

FAQ

Do I really have to give up my phone?

You don't give it up at all. You get a phone box to keep, tape your phone inside, and leave it in your room. We open all the boxes together on the last morning. It is an honor system, and honestly you won't miss it. The town is one street away and we hand you a paper map.

Do I need any art experience?

None at all. The drawing and collage sessions are guided and built for beginners, and everything else, from yoga to kayaking, is all-level and optional. This is for creative people getting away from screens, not a class you can fall behind in.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes and layers for cool mountain evenings, and anything you would like to draw with or make from: a sketchbook, old photos, magazines, maps. We provide pencils, paper, collage supplies, and the rest.

Are meals included?

No, and that is on purpose. Everybody eats differently, and the town is a block down the street with its own spots, so you get to do your own thing. Your room is booked directly with the lodge.

How do I get there?

Peaks of Otter Resort sits on the Blue Ridge Parkway near Bedford, Virginia. The nearest airport is Roanoke (ROA), roughly an hour away by car. We can help coordinate carpools once you register.

Is it actually tax-deductible?

Often, as professional development, but it depends on your work and your tax situation. We send an itemized receipt. Check with your accountant on what applies to you.
